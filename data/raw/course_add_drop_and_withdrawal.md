## 1. Introduction

At **Nova Crest Institute of Engineering (NCIE)**, course registration adjustments are managed through a structured academic workflow that allows students to modify their enrolled course list within defined academic timelines. These adjustments may include adding a course, dropping a course, or withdrawing from a course after initial registration.

The add, drop, and withdrawal processes ensure flexibility for students while maintaining academic record accuracy and alignment with assessment and examination systems.

This document describes how course enrollment changes are handled at NCIE during a semester.

## 2. Course Enrollment Context

At the start of each semester, students at NCIE enroll in courses as part of the academic registration process. Once enrolled, the course list becomes the basis for:

* Continuous Assessment activities
* Examination eligibility
* Academic performance tracking
* Credit accumulation

Any modification to course enrollment directly affects downstream academic processes and must therefore be recorded accurately.

## 3. Add Course Process

### 3.1 Purpose of Adding a Course

Adding a course allows students to enroll in an additional academic course after the initial registration phase. This may occur due to:

* Changes in academic planning
* Availability of elective slots
* Advisory recommendations

### 3.2 Add Course Workflow

The typical add course process at NCIE includes:

1. Student initiates an add request through the academic portal.
2. The system verifies eligibility and timetable compatibility.
3. Approval is reviewed by the **Faculty Advisor** or **Department Academic Coordinator**.
4. Upon approval, the course is added to the student’s enrollment record.
5. The academic system updates assessment and examination mappings accordingly.

Once added, the course becomes subject to Continuous Assessment and End-Semester Examination requirements.

## 4. Drop Course Process

### 4.1 Purpose of Dropping a Course

Dropping a course allows a student to remove a course from their enrollment early in the semester, typically when the course has not progressed significantly.

Dropping a course ensures that the student is not evaluated or examined for that course.

### 4.2 Drop Course Workflow

The standard drop process at NCIE includes:

1. Student submits a drop request through the academic portal.
2. The request is reviewed for academic feasibility.
3. Approval is obtained from the **Faculty Advisor** or **Department Academic Coordinator**.
4. Upon approval, the course is removed from the enrollment record.
5. Associated assessment and examination records are cleared from the system.

Dropped courses do not appear in the student’s academic performance records.

## 5. Course Withdrawal Process

### 5.1 Purpose of Course Withdrawal

Course withdrawal applies to situations where a student exits a course after the semester has progressed beyond the early adjustment phase. Withdrawal acknowledges partial participation without full academic evaluation.

### 5.2 Withdrawal Workflow

The withdrawal process at NCIE generally includes:

1. Student submits a withdrawal request through the academic portal.
2. The request is reviewed by academic authorities.
3. Supporting context or documentation may be requested.
4. Approval is coordinated by the department.
5. The withdrawal status is recorded in the academic system.

Withdrawn courses are reflected distinctly in academic records to indicate non-completion.

## 6. Impact on Continuous Assessment

Enrollment changes directly affect Continuous Assessment tracking:

* Added courses require initiation of CA components.
* Dropped courses remove CA obligations entirely.
* Withdrawn courses may retain partial CA records depending on timing.

The academic system ensures synchronization between enrollment status and assessment records.

## 7. Impact on Examination Registration

Course enrollment status determines examination eligibility:

* Added courses become eligible for exam registration.
* Dropped courses are excluded from exam registration.
* Withdrawn courses are not eligible for end-semester examination.

Students are advised to complete enrollment changes before initiating exam registration to avoid inconsistencies.

## 8. Role of Faculty and Departments

Academic roles involved in enrollment adjustments include:

* **Faculty Advisor**
  Provides guidance on academic planning and feasibility.

* **Department Academic Coordinator**
  Oversees approvals and ensures consistency across the department.

Departments ensure that enrollment changes do not adversely affect academic progression or system integrity.

## 9. System Updates and Data Integrity

Course add, drop, and withdrawal actions trigger updates across multiple academic systems, including:

* Course enrollment databases
* Continuous Assessment records
* Examination eligibility lists
* Academic performance summaries

Accurate and timely updates are essential to prevent discrepancies.

## 10. Common Scenarios and Clarifications

Common situations handled through these processes include:

* Switching between elective courses
* Adjusting workload due to academic difficulty
* Aligning courses with project or internship commitments

Students are encouraged to consult academic advisors before initiating enrollment changes.

## 11. Special Considerations

Certain academic contexts may require additional coordination, such as:

* Final-year project semesters
* Internship-aligned semesters
* Students with academic accommodations

These cases are handled through departmental review and documented explicitly.

## 12. Summary

At Nova Crest Institute of Engineering, course add, drop, and withdrawal processes provide structured flexibility within the academic system. Through coordinated workflows, these processes ensure accurate enrollment records, reliable assessment tracking, and alignment with examination and evaluation frameworks.
