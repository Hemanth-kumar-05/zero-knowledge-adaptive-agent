## 1. Introduction

At **Nova Crest Institute of Engineering (NCIE)**, grading is the academic mechanism through which student performance in a course is evaluated and recorded. Grades reflect a combination of continuous academic engagement and end-semester assessment outcomes.

The grading structure at NCIE is designed to ensure transparency, consistency, and alignment with learning objectives across courses and semesters. This document describes the components involved in grading and how weightage is applied within the academic evaluation framework.

## 2. Components of Course Evaluation

Course evaluation at NCIE is composed of two primary components:

* **Continuous Assessment (CA)**
  Ongoing evaluation conducted throughout the semester.

* **End-Semester Examination (ESE)**
  Comprehensive examination conducted at the end of the semester.

Together, these components determine the final academic outcome for a course.

## 3. Weightage Distribution

At NCIE, the standard weightage distribution for regular academic courses is:

* **Continuous Assessment (CA): 40%**
* **End-Semester Examination (ESE): 60%**

This distribution applies uniformly across undergraduate courses unless a course is explicitly designed with a different evaluation structure (such as project-only or laboratory-only modules).

## 4. Continuous Assessment Contribution

The **40% Continuous Assessment** component may include one or more of the following, depending on the course design:

* Mid-semester tests or internal examinations
* Assignments and quizzes
* Laboratory performance and internal marks
* Project reviews or coursework evaluations

Marks from these components are accumulated over the semester and recorded in the academic system before end-semester examinations.

## 5. End-Semester Examination Contribution

The **60% End-Semester Examination** component typically involves:

* A written examination conducted at the end of the semester
* Evaluation based on syllabus coverage and learning outcomes
* Centralized scheduling and evaluation coordinated by the Examination Cell

Only students who have completed required academic processes, including exam registration, are considered for ESE evaluation.

## 6. Integration of CA and ESE Scores

Final course evaluation involves combining verified CA and ESE scores according to the prescribed weightage. The integrated score forms the basis for determining the course grade.

The integration process occurs only after:

* CA marks are finalized and verified
* ESE results are declared or updated (including revaluation, where applicable)

## 7. Grading Scale and Grade Representation

NCIE uses a **letter-grade system** to represent academic outcomes. Letter grades indicate performance ranges and are recorded in the academic system.

Grades are used for:

* Academic performance tracking
* Progression evaluation
* Program completion verification

Numerical computation details, where applicable, are managed internally within the academic system.

## 8. Special Course Categories

Certain academic components may follow modified grading practices, including:

* **Laboratory-only courses**
  Emphasis on internal and practical evaluation.

* **Project modules**
  Evaluation through reviews and submissions as part of Continuous Assessment.

* **Internship components**
  Evaluated based on completion and academic review, without theory-based grading.

These variations remain aligned with the overall academic framework.

## 9. Role of Academic Offices and Faculty

Grading at NCIE involves coordinated responsibilities:

* **Course Instructors**
  Conduct evaluation and submit Continuous Assessment marks.

* **Department Academic Coordinators**
  Verify grading consistency and completeness.

* **Examination Cell**
  Manages End-Semester Examination evaluation and result processing.

Final grades are published only after verification across these roles.

## 10. Revisions and Result Updates

In cases where evaluation outcomes change due to processes such as revaluation:

* Updated ESE scores replace previous records
* Integrated grades are recalculated
* Revised grades are reflected in the academic system

Once updated, grades are treated as final for academic purposes.

## 11. Academic Record Integrity

NCIE emphasizes integrity and accuracy in grading by ensuring:

* Timely recording of evaluation data
* Verification before result publication
* Controlled updates to academic records
* Traceability of grading components

Students are encouraged to review academic records and report discrepancies through established academic channels.

## 12. System Dependencies

The grading framework at NCIE depends on synchronized operation of:

* Continuous Assessment tracking systems
* Examination evaluation databases
* Academic performance management modules
* Student academic portals

System consistency ensures reliable grade computation and reporting.

## 13. Summary

At Nova Crest Institute of Engineering, grading is a structured integration of Continuous Assessment and End-Semester Examination outcomes. With a **40% CA and 60% ESE** distribution, the grading framework supports comprehensive evaluation of student learning while maintaining consistency, transparency, and academic integrity across programs.
